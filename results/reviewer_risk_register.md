## Highest-Risk Reviewer Attacks

- Small sample: the fully audited headline LLM result is 150 examples, split 75/75 across synthetic clinical and TAB-derived legal data, with three CSG strict-specificity audited-loss rows and 11 residual CSG QI rows.
- Sample-size sensitivity: a no-API n200 local/oracle diagnostic preserves the local frontier, but it does not replace a 300-500 example fully audited non-oracle headline.
- Clinical realism: the clinical split is synthetic and should be framed as controlled utility evidence, not real-note validation.
- Legal annotation quality: legal facts are heuristic plus spot-checks, not expert legal annotation.
- Judge dependence: audited TCFR is useful for paraphrase, but it is still an LLM judge and needs manual examples.
- Method attribution: the current ablation supports the deterministic safety/repair layer, not a verifier-prompt gain.
- Privacy claim scope: measured direct/QI checks are not formal anonymization or adversarial privacy guarantees.

## Recommended One-Sentence Claim

In a 150-example controlled clinical/legal run, CSG improves the observed privacy-utility tradeoff by reducing privacy risk relative to generic prompting and preserving task-critical facts relative to privacy-first prompting, with explicit residual legal caveats.
